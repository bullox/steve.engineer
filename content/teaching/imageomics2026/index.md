## Before the session

Allow about an hour for my part, detail on this page. Justin's and Devis's preparation also linked from the

### 1. Get your drone basics

*About 30 minutes, if you might fly a drone in your research.*

:::important[You are responsible for your tech]
This is my reading of the rules as they stand in October 2026, not legal
advice. Drone rules change, and differ between countries. It is up to
you to operate within the requirements of the place you fly and of your own
institution, which may be stricter than the law.
:::

Even if you never fly one yourself, you will likely work with people who do, and the
test teaches you how airspace works. Choose the one for where you live or
will fly:

| Where | What to do | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **UK** | Get a free [Flyer ID](https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/getting-started-with-drones-and-model-aircraft/get-a-flyer-id/) from the CAA by passing the online theory test, and read [the Drone and Model Aircraft Code](https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/drone-code/). | Since 1 January 2026 you need a Flyer ID for any drone of 100 g or more. You'll also need an [Operator ID](https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/getting-started-with-drones-and-model-aircraft/get-an-operator-id/) before you fly one with a camera: [who needs which](https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/drone-code/getting-what-you-need-to-fly-legally/). |
| **EU** | Register as an operator and pass the free [A1/A3 online training and exam](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/drones-air-mobility/operating-drone/open-category-low-risk-civil-drones) with the aviation authority of the country you live in. EASA lists [each country's authority](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/naa). | One registration covers every EU country, plus Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland. Use the official national site: there are fake "licence" sites. |
| **US** | Take the free [Recreational UAS Safety Test (TRUST)](https://www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers/knowledge_test_updates). I recommend [Embry-Riddle's](https://webforms.erau.edu/public/faa-trust/); the FAA page lists the other approved providers. | **TRUST is for flying under the recreational exception.** Most research flying needs a [Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot); universities have a narrow exception, below. TRUST is a good foundation either way. |

The UK and EU do not distinguish between recreational and commercial flying:
the same rules apply whatever your purpose, so these qualifications are a real
first step for research flying in the Open category.

The US does distinguish. Training courses, Embry-Riddle's included, tell you
that if you're not flying for fun you need Part 107, and for most people that
is right. There is one exception for universities, explained in the
[reading](/teaching/imageomics2026/reading/#us-recreational-or-part-107): in
short, research by a university can count as "recreational", but only within
all the recreational limits, and many universities require Part 107 anyway.
Ask yours before you fly.

Then **get familiar with an airspace app** for where you'll fly. Open it, find
your home and your field site, and see what restrictions apply:

- **UK:** the CAA's [airspace restrictions](https://www.caa.co.uk/drones/open-category/moving-on-to-more-advanced-flying/airspace/airspace-restrictions/)
  page and the official [NATS UAS restriction zones](https://nats-uk.ead-it.com/cms-nats/opencms/en/uas-restriction-zones/).
  The CAA doesn't endorse an app; whichever you use, check it against these.
- **EU:** your country's geographical zones map, linked from EASA's
  [where to fly](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/drones-air-mobility/operating-drone/where-fly-easa-member-states-geo-zones)
  page. Examples: [DIPUL](https://maptool-dipul.dfs.de/) (Germany),
  [d-flight](https://www.d-flight.it/new_portal/) (Italy),
  [GoDrone](https://map.godrone.nl/) (Netherlands).
- **US:** a [B4UFLY](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/b4ufly) app,
  such as Aloft or Airspace Link. The same apps request
  [LAANC](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc) authorisation in
  controlled airspace.

### 3. Start your capability audit canvas

*About 20 minutes.*

Think about a system you use or plan to use in your research: drones, camera
traps, acoustic recorders, eDNA, satellite imagery, and the models that turn
their data into results. Fill in **parts 1 to 5** of the canvas: your mission,
the capabilities your system needs and how each could be misused, your
stakeholders, the permissions and risks that apply, and a question to bring.
Leave **part 6, the red team,** for the session.

[Capability audit canvas (.docx)](/teaching/imageomics2026/imageomics-capability-canvas.docx) ·
[Printable canvas (.pdf)](/teaching/imageomics2026/imageomics-capability-canvas.pdf)

Be specific. "Thermal camera at 120 m picks out people in the village next to
the reserve" is more useful than "privacy".

**Upload your canvas** before the session, as the completed .docx or a photo
or scan of the printed one, named `yourname-canvas`:
**upload link to follow**.

## Going further

- Read [Sandbrook et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1111/csp2.374), principles
  for the socially responsible use of conservation monitoring technology and
  data.
- Find your institution's ethics process and fieldwork risk assessment, and
  check whether your planned work needs them. Module 7,
  Safe and Inclusive Field Work (see the [syllabus](https://imageomics-abc-edu.github.io/AI-Ecology-2026/syllabus/syllabus/)),
  takes field safety further before you travel.
- If you'll fly for research in the US, start on
  [Part 107](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot). Check with your institution about expectations and funding.
